# Quantitative revision of *Dickinsonia* from the Central Urals

**Status:** Published collaborative research and dissertation work. The portfolio page should link to the final articles and distinguish the author's contribution from the team's taxonomic result.

**One-sentence card copy:** Designed and carried out comparative morphometric and statistical analyses in R to test whether Central Ural *Dickinsonia* specimens form a distinct morphological group.

## Research question

Do the Ural fossils fall within the variation of previously described *Dickinsonia* forms, or do their combined morphological traits support a distinct morphotype? This required comparing measurements, preservation, and size effects rather than relying on appearance alone.

## Data and approach

- Digital measurements of fossil impressions from the Central Urals, compared with Australian material.
- Main Ural collection: 170 specimens. Two comparative analysis subsets used 213 and 205 specimens with different sets of available traits; these are not independent study populations.
- Cleaning and log transformation of morphometric measurements; PCA to examine correlated variation; Gaussian-mixture clustering with BIC/ICL to compare models; non-parametric tests for diagnostic traits.
- Additional size-structure and spatial point-pattern analyses of the Ural assemblage.

## Evidence and interpretation

In the principal five-trait analysis, the selected mixture model contained three components. The Ural specimens formed a coherent part of one statistical group, with some Australian specimens overlapping it. Module density and deltoidal-region proportions contributed to the biological interpretation. A cluster is evidence about morphometric structure; the taxonomic conclusion also required comparative morphology and published description.

## Personal contribution

I participated in field and collection work, planned the quantitative comparisons, prepared and checked measurements, worked with R code, performed the statistical analyses and visualizations, and interpreted the results. The species description and publications have multiple authors; I do not claim sole authorship of the taxonomic result.

## Limits to retain on the page

- Preservation and deformation can alter measurements.
- The nine-trait variant was less stable than the principal five-trait analysis.
- Overlap between regions means the statistical separation is not absolute.
- Ecological explanations for size and spatial patterns are interpretations, not direct observations.
